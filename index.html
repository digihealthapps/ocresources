Field Focus

Wherever you sit in healthcare

Most of this hub applies across roles. These three notes speak more directly to where you sit — most members today are in design and business development, and we want providers joining us to see themselves here too.

Design & the built environment

Architects, planners, interior designers, and facilities leaders shaping how care spaces work. The leadership drop-off here is steeper than in medicine: about half of architecture students are women, but only 17% of registered architects and 16% of AEC firm C-suites. Advocacy often centers on who gets named principal-in-charge, who leads client-facing pitches, and whose design rationale survives value engineering.

See design & AEC organizations →

Business development & sales leadership

Vendor relations, procurement, capital equipment and services sales, marketing, and growth roles. Advocacy here often centers on territory and account assignment, commission and quota structures, who gets face time with health-system decision-makers, and getting credit for revenue you generated when a deal closes under someone else's name.

See pitch & negotiation scripts →

Clinical practice & provider roles

Physicians, APPs, nurses, and allied health providers. If you're newer to this community: welcome. Advocacy here often centers on pay equity by specialty, promotion and academic rank, scheduling and call equity, and being heard in clinical decision-making.

Self

Advocate for yourself

Negotiation, promotion, and self-advocacy are learnable skills, not personality traits. These resources focus on preparation, not confrontation.

Skill-building

Salary & offer negotiation

Before any conversation: gather comparable-role salary data (state hospital association surveys, MGMA/AMGA compensation reports if your specialty is covered, Glassdoor/Levels-style aggregators as a sanity check — not a source of record), write your ask as a single sentence, and practice it out loud before you need it.

Skill-building

The promotion case, not the performance review

Performance reviews document the past; promotion cases argue for the future. Keep a running document of scope changes, outcomes you've owned, and problems you've solved that weren't technically your job — this becomes the raw material for both.

Skill-building

Naming a pattern without over-explaining it

The strongest self-advocacy language is short, specific, and forward-looking: what happened, what you want instead, and one concrete next step. See Templates & Scripts for ready language.

Skill-building

Getting named on the project, not just doing the work

For design and BD members: track which projects and closed deals you actually drove, not just supported. Principal-in-charge, project lead, and named-on-the-pitch are titles worth asking for explicitly — they rarely get offered by default.

Skill-building

Negotiating fee, scope, and commission structures

Scope creep on a project and unclear commission or quota structures in sales both quietly erode compensation over time. Ask for the underlying formula in writing, not just the number — it's easier to negotiate a variable you can see.

Others

Advocate for others

Sponsorship moves someone forward when they're not in the room. Allyship is most useful when it's specific, consistent, and doesn't require the person being supported to ask for it.

Practice

Mentorship vs. sponsorship

A mentor gives advice; a sponsor spends their own credibility putting someone's name forward for a room, a project, or a promotion they don't yet know is possible. Both matter — sponsorship is the one healthcare organizations under-invest in.

Practice

Interrupting being talked over or credit-skimmed

A short, repeatable move: redirect credit in real time ("building on what [name] just said…") and follow up privately afterward. Consistency matters more than any single intervention.

Structure

Employee resource groups & peer pods

Small, standing peer groups (4–6 people, recurring monthly) tend to outperform large ERGs for candid problem-solving. See Build the Case for a pitch template if your organization doesn't have one yet.

Case

Build the business case

Equity initiatives get funded when they're presented using metrics that leadership already uses: retention, patient outcomes, risk, and cost.

Angle

Retention & attrition cost

Attrition among women in healthcare has plateaued rather than improved industry-wide, even with current representation statistics. Pair a national stat with your own organization's trends if you have access to that data.

Angle

Patient outcomes

Published research links gender-diverse leadership teams to stronger performance on preventive care and chronic disease management measures. Useful in a business case, but cite the original study rather than a secondhand summary when it goes in front of leadership.

Structure

The one-page pitch

Problem (with your org's own data if you have it) → what exists elsewhere → proposed pilot, scoped small → cost and owner → what success looks like in two quarters. Leadership funds pilots more easily than programs.

Angle

Who gets on the pitch team

For design and BD leads building a case internally: client and RFP win rates, not just headcount, are the metric that moves leadership. If you can show that mixed-gender pitch teams or account teams close at parity or better, that's the argument — pair it with your own firm's pipeline data where you have it.

Rights

Know your rights

A general orientation to the U.S. legal landscape around pay and workplace equity. This is background, not legal advice — see the notice below.

Not legal advice. Laws vary by state and situation changes outcomes. If you're facing a specific issue — pay discrimination, retaliation, harassment, a leave-related dispute — talk to an employment attorney or your state labor agency before acting. Many offer free initial consultations.
Federal

Equal Pay Act & Title VII

The Equal Pay Act prohibits gender-based pay differences for substantially equal work; Title VII of the Civil Rights Act prohibits broader employment discrimination based on sex, including pregnancy. The EEOC (eeoc.gov) is the federal agency that enforces both and accepts individual complaints.

eeoc.gov — equal pay & pay transparency →
State

Pay transparency & salary history laws

A growing number of states and cities require salary ranges in job postings or prohibit employers from asking about salary history. Rules differ significantly by location — worth checking your state department of labor before a negotiation.

Leave

FMLA & pregnancy accommodation

The federal Family and Medical Leave Act and the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act cover job-protected leave and workplace accommodations in many circumstances. Coverage depends on employer size and tenure — the U.S. Department of Labor's site has current eligibility rules.

dol.gov — FMLA overview →
Tools

Templates & scripts

Language you can adapt in just a few minutes.

Email

Requesting a pay equity review

"I'd like to schedule time to review my compensation as compared to [role/level] benchmarks. I've attached the data I'm using and I'm happy to walk through it together."

In the room

Redirecting credit

"Adding to what [name] mentioned a minute ago, I think that's a direction worth pursuing." Said once, it's generous. Said consistently, it's a pattern people notice.

1:1

Naming being talked over

"I've noticed my points sometimes get revisited after someone else raises them. I wanted to name that directly, not to relitigate any one meeting."

Proposal opener

Pitching a new program

"Here's what we're seeing in our own data, here's a scoped 90-day pilot, and here's how we'll know if it's working." Leads with evidence, ends with an ask that's easy to say yes to.

Agenda

First ERG / peer-pod meeting

Welcome & purpose (5 min) → round of one-sentence check-ins (10 min) → one shared topic (25 min) → one commitment each before next time (5 min). Repeatable, low-prep, high-trust.

Documentation

Incident log format

Date, what was said/done verbatim if possible, who else was present, and what you did next. A private log — kept outside work systems — can make a significant difference between "I feel like" and "here's what happened."

Design / BD

Requesting a seat on the pitch team

"I've led the design work on [project/account] from the start and I'd like to be in the room for the client pitch, not just supporting it beforehand."

Design / BD

Pushing back on scope creep or value engineering

"This change affects [outcome the client cares about] and I want to flag it now before it becomes a problem later. Here's what I'd propose instead."
